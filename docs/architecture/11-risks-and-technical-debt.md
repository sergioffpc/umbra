# 11. Risks and Technical Debt

Risks follow from decisions already taken; each names what would show it is happening and what limits the damage. Probability and impact are the owner's judgement: high, medium or low. The list is reviewed when a milestone closes, together with its benchmark and validation issues.

## 11.1 Risks

| Id    | Risk                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Probability         | Impact                                                                     | Early sign                                                                                                                                 | Mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RK-1  | **Determinism costs too much speed.** Explicit FMA, no flush-to-zero, no vendor-dependent approximate reciprocals and one BVH layout everywhere (ADR 0022, §8.14) may keep QS-5 below 1.0.                                                                         | Medium              | High: v1.0.0 cannot close                                                  | QS-5 below 0.5 at a milestone, or profiles dominated by `libm` calls or denormals                                                          | The tuning backlog (§8.14); ADR 0022 already allows the fastest transcendentals and reordered SIMD within stated error bounds (§8.11).                                                                           |
| RK-3  | **Large Scenes are slow to replicate or do not fit.** A Worker receives over 1 GbE every asset its Asset Cache lacks, the whole Scene the first time it sees it (ADR 0016, ADR 0030), and a machine with several NUMA nodes holds one replica per node (ADR 0017). | Medium, from v0.5.0 | Medium: Scene transfer dominates short Render Jobs; a Worker is left out   | Workers shown as `loading` on the status line (§8.10) for a time comparable to rendering; QS-7 below 90 %; a Worker exits on out of memory | Run Render Jobs with large Scenes from the strongest machine (§7.3). The Asset Cache spares repeated transfers, bounded by its size (ADR 0030). An in-process NUMA replica (§8.14, entry 2) is a later decision. |
| RK-4  | **Unsupported asset formats.** Later milestones need decoders for EXR, PLY, Ptex, NanoVDB and measured-BRDF files. Pure-Rust, MIT-compatible, maintained crates may not exist for every one.                                                                       | Medium              | Medium: a milestone needs its own decoder or C bindings                    | No suitable crate when a milestone is planned                                                                                              | Decided milestone by milestone when that milestone is planned (§4.1); writing a decoder is in scope, out-of-core is not.                                                                                         |
| RK-5  | **Splatting and Markov-chain integrators strain the Work Unit model.** BDPT and light tracing splat across Tiles, and MLT and SPPM do not map directly onto Tiles × Passes, while each Pixel Sample must stay deterministic on one machine (ADR 0022).             | High, from v0.11.0  | High: a redesign of scheduling or of the Work Result                       | The open questions on the implementation-plan map                                                                                          | Already known as open work on the map; the splat accumulator is merged on arrival (§8.3). Decided before v0.11.0 is planned.                                                                                     |
| RK-6  | **Reference renders are expensive.** QS-1 needs a Reference Image at 64 × a Reference Scene's Pixel Samples per pixel, plus 16 seeds for validation, all rendered by pbrt-v4 on the target machines.                                                               | Medium              | Medium: milestones wait on reference renders                               | Reference renders of a milestone's new Scenes taking days                                                                                  | Rendered once per pinned commit and published as release assets (§8.11); moving the pinned commit is an explicit, rare decision.                                                                                 |
| RK-7  | **Statistical tests give false verdicts.** The per-pixel Welch test with Benjamini–Hochberg may flag correct images or miss small biases.                                                                                                                          | Low                 | Medium: blocked milestones, or a bias that ships                           | A rejection that no Pixel Trace explains; QS-1 failing with a passing Welch test                                                           | Two independent checks block a milestone (Welch for bias, relMSE for noise, §8.11); sampling routines have their own χ² tests.                                                                                   |
| RK-8  | **One person carries the whole plan.** Fourteen milestones on two Render Devices, measurement and validation by hand on two machines.                                                                                                                              | High                | Medium: slower progress, not wrong results                                 | Milestones open for much longer than earlier ones                                                                                          | Work split into `ready-for-agent` issues with no open decision (§4.2); decisions recorded in ADRs and this document so that any contributor can continue.                                                        |
| RK-9  | **The shared core does not fit the GPU well.** One core under two drivers (ADR 0028) may diverge badly in `match` dispatch on the GPU, or need CPU-hostile changes to reach good occupancy.                                                                        | Medium              | Medium: core changes that slow the CPU, or a GPU far below its potential   | Low GPU occupancy or high divergence in Nsight Compute; GPU speed close to the CPU's on the same machine                                   | The rules are exercised from v0.1.0 (ADR 0026), so the core is still small when they fail; material sorting and `TextureEvaluator` stay ready in the wavefront (§8.12).                                          |
| RK-10 | **A nightly bump breaks the build or the code.** The whole workspace uses a pinned nightly (ADR 0027), and `umbra-kernels` relies on unstable features that may change.                                                                                            | Medium              | Low: a bump is delayed or needs rework in `umbra-kernels`                  | CI fails on the bump commit; an unstable feature changes its syntax                                                                        | Bumps are deliberate, in a commit of their own; only `umbra-kernels` enables unstable features.                                                                                                                  |
| RK-11 | **GPU regressions are found late.** CI has no GPU (§2.2), so a pull request can break the GPU Worker unnoticed.                                                                                                                                                    | High                | Medium: a milestone's closing validation finds several regressions at once | The GPU test script fails at a milestone's close                                                                                           | CI still compiles the kernels for `nvptx64`; the script can be run by hand on the P620 before merging any change to the core or the GPU crates (§8.12).                                                          |
| RK-12 | **OptiX from Rust is fragile.** The OptiX device API is reached through inline PTX (`asm!`, unstable on `nvptx64`), with bindings written by hand against the OptiX headers.                                                                                       | Medium              | High: GPU ray tracing blocked, or a fall back to a software BVH on the GPU | The tracer bullet of v0.1.0 (§4.2) cannot trace a triangle through OptiX                                                                   | The tracer bullet comes first in v0.1.0, so this shows before any feature depends on it; the headers' licence is checked there too.                                                                              |
