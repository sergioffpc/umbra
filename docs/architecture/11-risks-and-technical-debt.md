# 11. Risks and Technical Debt

Risks follow from decisions already taken; each names what would show it is happening and what limits the damage. Probability and impact are the owner's judgement: high, medium or low. The list is reviewed when a milestone closes, together with its benchmark and validation issues.

## 11.1 Risks

| Id   | Risk                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Probability         | Impact                                                                   | Early sign                                                                                                                                 | Mitigation                                                                                                                                                |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RK-1 | **Determinism costs too much speed.** Explicit FMA, no flush-to-zero, no vendor-dependent approximate reciprocals and one BVH layout everywhere (ADR 0022, §8.14) may keep QS-5 below 1.0.                                                             | Medium              | High: v1.0.0 cannot close                                                | QS-5 below 0.5 at a milestone, or profiles dominated by `libm` calls or denormals                                                          | The tuning backlog (§8.14); ADR 0022 already allows the fastest transcendentals and reordered SIMD within stated error bounds (§8.11).                    |
| RK-3 | **Large Scenes are slow to replicate or do not fit.** Every Worker receives the whole Scene over 1 GbE in each Render Job, with no asset cache (ADR 0016), and a machine with several NUMA nodes holds one replica per node (ADR 0017).                | Medium, from v0.6.0 | Medium: Scene transfer dominates short Render Jobs; a Worker is left out | Workers shown as `loading` on the status line (§8.10) for a time comparable to rendering; QS-7 below 90 %; a Worker exits on out of memory | Run Render Jobs with large Scenes from the strongest machine (§7.3). An asset cache or an in-process NUMA replica (§8.14, entry 2) are later decisions.   |
| RK-4 | **Unsupported asset formats.** Later milestones need decoders for EXR, PLY, Ptex, NanoVDB and measured-BRDF files. Pure-Rust, MIT-compatible, maintained crates may not exist for every one.                                                           | Medium              | Medium: a milestone needs its own decoder or C bindings                  | No suitable crate when a milestone is planned                                                                                              | Decided milestone by milestone when that milestone is planned (§4.1); writing a decoder is in scope, out-of-core is not.                                  |
| RK-5 | **Splatting and Markov-chain integrators strain the Work Unit model.** BDPT and light tracing splat across Tiles, and MLT and SPPM do not map directly onto Tiles × Passes, while each Pixel Sample must stay deterministic on one machine (ADR 0022). | High, from v0.12.0  | High: a redesign of scheduling or of the Work Result                     | The open questions on the implementation-plan map                                                                                          | Already known as open work on the map; the splat accumulator is merged on arrival (§8.3). Decided before v0.12.0 is planned.                              |
| RK-6 | **Reference renders are expensive.** QS-1 needs a Reference Image at 64 × a Reference Scene's Pixel Samples per pixel, plus 16 seeds for validation, all rendered by pbrt-v4 on the target machines.                                                   | Medium              | Medium: milestones wait on reference renders                             | Reference renders of a milestone's new Scenes taking days                                                                                  | Rendered once per pinned commit and published as release assets (§8.11); moving the pinned commit is an explicit, rare decision.                          |
| RK-7 | **Statistical tests give false verdicts.** The per-pixel Welch test with Benjamini–Hochberg may flag correct images or miss small biases.                                                                                                              | Low                 | Medium: blocked milestones, or a bias that ships                         | A rejection that no Pixel Trace explains; QS-1 failing with a passing Welch test                                                           | Two independent checks block a milestone (Welch for bias, relMSE for noise, §8.11); sampling routines have their own χ² tests.                            |
| RK-8 | **One person carries the whole plan.** Fifteen milestones, measurement and validation by hand on two machines.                                                                                                                                         | High                | Medium: slower progress, not wrong results                               | Milestones open for much longer than earlier ones                                                                                          | Work split into `ready-for-agent` issues with no open decision (§4.2); decisions recorded in ADRs and this document so that any contributor can continue. |
| RK-9 | **The GPU-readiness rules are the wrong ones.** They are chosen for today's Rust GPU toolchains, which are immature.                                                                                                                                   | Medium              | Medium: the GPU milestone after v1.0.0 needs a rewrite of core code      | —                                                                                                                                          | The rules cost little on CPU (§8.12); anything heavier (wavefront driver, SoA) is deferred to the GPU milestone (ADR 0024).                               |
