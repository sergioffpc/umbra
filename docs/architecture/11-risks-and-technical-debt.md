# 11. Risks and Technical Debt

Risks follow from decisions already taken; each names what would show it is happening and what limits the damage. Probability and impact are the owner's judgement: high, medium or low. The list is reviewed when a milestone closes, together with its benchmark and validation issues.

## 11.1 Risks

| Id | Risk | Probability | Impact | Early sign | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RK-1 | **Determinism costs too much speed.** Portable `libm`, explicit FMA, no flush-to-zero, no approximate reciprocals and one BVH layout everywhere (ADR 0002, ADR 0004, §8.14) may keep QS-5 below 1.0. | Medium | High: v1.0.0 cannot close | QS-5 below 0.5 at a milestone, or profiles dominated by `libm` calls or denormals | The tuning backlog (§8.14); exact optimisations that keep operation order are still allowed (§8.11). Relaxing ADR 0002 would be a new ADR, weighed against Correctness, which ranks higher. |
| RK-2 | **Bit-identity breaks across arm64 and x86-64.** A compiler upgrade, a dependency (`wide`, `libm`) or a new kernel changes the last bit on one architecture. | Medium | Medium: QS-2 fails, reassignment is no longer exact | A golden Film hash differs between the arm64 and x86-64 CI jobs | Golden hashes on both architectures in CI (§8.11), the pinned toolchain (§2.1), and a lint against non-portable maths (§8.6). |
| RK-3 | **Large Scenes are slow to replicate or do not fit.** Every Worker receives the whole Scene over 1 GbE or Wi-Fi in each Session, with no asset cache (ADR 0016), and the R630 holds two replicas (ADR 0017). The MacBook has 32 GB. | Medium, from v0.6.0 | Medium: Scene transfer dominates short Render Jobs; a Worker is left out | Workers shown as `loading` on the status line (§8.10) for a time comparable to rendering; QS-7 below 90 %; a Worker exits on out of memory | Run Sessions with large Scenes from the wired P620 (§7.3); leave the MacBook out of them. An asset cache or an in-process NUMA replica (§8.14, entry 2) are later decisions. |
| RK-4 | **Unsupported asset formats.** Later milestones need decoders for EXR, PLY, Ptex, NanoVDB and measured-BRDF files. Pure-Rust, MIT-compatible, maintained crates may not exist for every one. | Medium | Medium: a milestone needs its own decoder or C bindings | No suitable crate when a milestone is planned | Decided milestone by milestone when that milestone is planned (§4.1); writing a decoder is in scope, out-of-core and GPU are not. |
| RK-5 | **Splatting and Markov-chain integrators strain the Work Unit model.** BDPT and light tracing splat across Tiles, and MLT and SPPM do not map directly onto Tiles × Passes, while the Film must stay deterministic (ADR 0004). | High, from v0.12.0 | High: a redesign of scheduling or of the Work Result | The open questions on the implementation-plan map | Already known as open work on the map; the splat accumulator is merged in a fixed (Pass, Tile) order (§8.3). Decided before v0.12.0 is planned. |
| RK-6 | **Reference renders are expensive.** QS-1 needs a Reference Image at 64 × a Reference Scene's Pixel Samples per pixel, plus 16 seeds for validation, all rendered by pbrt-v4 on the target machines. | Medium | Medium: milestones wait on reference renders | Reference renders of a milestone's new Scenes taking days | Rendered once per pinned commit and published as release assets (§8.11); moving the pinned commit is an explicit, rare decision. |
| RK-7 | **Statistical tests give false verdicts.** The per-pixel Welch test with Benjamini–Hochberg may flag correct images or miss small biases. | Low | Medium: blocked milestones, or a bias that ships | A rejection that no Pixel Trace explains; QS-1 failing with a passing Welch test | Two independent checks block a milestone (Welch for bias, relMSE for noise, §8.11); sampling routines have their own χ² tests. |
| RK-8 | **One person carries the whole plan.** Fifteen milestones, measurement and validation by hand on three machines. | High | Medium: slower progress, not wrong results | Milestones open for much longer than earlier ones | Work split into `ready-for-agent` issues with no open decision (§4.2); decisions recorded in ADRs and this document so that any contributor can continue. |
| RK-9 | **The GPU-readiness rules are the wrong ones.** They are chosen for today's Rust GPU toolchains, which are immature. | Medium | Low: GPU is out of scope | — | The rules cost little on CPU (§8.12); anything heavier (wavefront driver, SoA) is deferred until a GPU effort exists. |

## 11.2 Technical debt

| Id | Debt | Why it exists | Repayment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TD-1 | **The release pipeline publishes one `x86-64-v3` image.** The `Dockerfile`, `.github/workflows/release-image.yml`, `README.md` and the "Release container image" section of `CONTRIBUTING.md` still describe a single `linux/amd64` image with `latest` tags, while [ADR 0012](../adr/0012-per-cpu-binaries-locked-to-one-commit.md) requires one image per CPU (`znver2`, `broadwell`) and drops `latest`. The VS Code task `build: portable release (x86-64-v3)` builds the generic binary that ADR 0012 rules out. | The pipeline predates the per-CPU decision. | Before the first release tag, `v0.1.0`: `TARGET_CPU` build argument, one image per Linux CPU, README and CONTRIBUTING updated, the portable VS Code task removed. |
| TD-2 | **The README says nothing renders yet** and gives a `cargo run` with no subcommand. | No code exists yet. | With the `umbra` binary issue of v0.1.0 (§4.2). |

Nothing else is owed yet: no rendering code has been written. From the first milestone on, every exception to the rules of §8 (an `#[expect]` on a lint, an `unsafe` block, a deferred test) is recorded here with its repayment, or justified in place where the rule allows it.
